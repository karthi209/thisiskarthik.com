  1. Jul 19 sun

    we went to see the odyssey

    i was supposed to watch the odyssey with a friend from work last friday, but work decided it would rather have my company.

    so today, dad and i finally made it to ags navalur inside vivera mall.

    the movie was brilliant.

    the theatre deserves considerably fewer compliments.

    i don't know what was going on with the audio in screen 4, but it sounded like someone had put a blanket over the speakers. for a christopher nolan film where sound is half the experience that's almost a crime. i was already down that i couldn't get tickts in imax, but this was beyond that.

    anyway, future me:

    never book ags navalur, screen 4 again.

  2. Jul 16 thu

    an afternoon with old stories

    to sort out some land business, dad and i drove down to an old family village. apparently a tiny piece of my family's history has been sitting there all this time.

    the story goes that my grandmother's father was from this village. he worked as a driver in singapore for years, saved up enough money and came back to buy quite a bit of land around the village. it's honestly funny to think that working as a driver back then could buy you acres of land. today you'd be lucky to buy a parking spot.

    unfortunately, he passed away not long after. from what i've always heard, my great-grandmother was far too trusting, and the "oor kaaranga" somehow convinced her to give up a good portion of the land. nobody in the family seems to know exactly how it happened anymore. the story has changed a little every time i've heard it. thankfully, the documents didn't disappear along with it.

    so, about fifty years later, dad and i found ourselves driving around the same village trying to locate land that neither of us had ever seen before. the plan was to find the land, finalize the sale and head home before dinner.

    naturally, none of that happened.

    we had a buyer waiting, but after a lot of discussion we couldn't agree on the price, so the deal fell through. in the end, all we really accomplished was proving that land disputes can outlive the people involved.

    since we were already in the area, we decided to visit a few relatives nearby. from there we drove to another village to meet my grandmother's uncle.

    this is where my family tree completely stops making sense.

    my grandmother is in her seventies.

    her uncle is barely a year older.

    apparently, when they were young, my great-grandmother raised him because he was the youngest child in a very large family. that was fairly common back then, with older siblings often becoming a second set of parents.

    we ended up talking to him for almost two hours.

    his story was actually quite heartbreaking.

    being the youngest in the family meant he spent his entire life working on the family's farm. he did the hard labor, but when it came time to divide the property, there wasn't much left for him. every time he tried to leave the village or find a different job, his older siblings would convince him to stay because there was always more work to be done.

    somehow, decades passed like that.

    at one point he smiled and said something that has stayed with me ever since.

    "i'm sitting here exactly as i was born. i don't have anything more... just the clothes i'm wearing."

    it's one thing to hear stories about family history, but it's another to sit across from someone who lived through it. all of a sudden, the land we had been arguing about the whole day didn't seem nearly as important anymore.

  3. Feb 15 sun

    i went to see cricket for the first time

    for the first time ever, i went to watch a cricket match live in a stadium.

    i never realized how enormous a cricket stadium feels until i was sitting inside one. the grass was greener than it looked on television, the sky was perfectly blue, and the atmosphere was incredible.

    i had an absolute blast.
