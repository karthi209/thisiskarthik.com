# Site editorial and privacy guidance

This is a personal notebook that happens to be publicly readable: “for later,
when i forget.” Keep highly personal stories with low real-world identifiability.
Apply this across timeline, essays, games, books, photos, about, metadata, feeds,
structured data, and future sections. Preserve the quiet dark notebook aesthetic: IBM Plex typography, compact
layouts, thin borders, and small offset frames.

Keep experiences, interests, opinions, family relationships, borrowed objects,
and uncertain memories. Prefer “my dad”, “my grandmother”, or “a friend” over
names. Preserve approximate dates and unknown details; never invent precision,
specifications, identities, game titles, or images that imply fake history.

Omit or generalize unnecessary full names, home locations, recurring routes,
contact details, employers/teams, school identifiers, account identifiers,
licence plates, personal documents, receipts, and links to identity profiles.
Do not expose information just because it exists in APIs, source files,
configuration, image metadata, or Git history. Keep provider IDs internal.
The existing understated first-name byline does not authorize additional identity
information. Do not strip personality or public place names that matter to a story.

Prefer photographs of objects. Review the specific image before publication.
Recognizable people, private surroundings, account information, identifying
papers, addresses, or licence plates require explicit approval for that image;
existing availability or an earlier general request for photos is not approval.
Text-only entries are welcome. Never fill a missing historical photograph with
stock or generated imagery presented as the actual object.

Normalize photo orientation before upload and strip unnecessary EXIF/GPS, XMP,
IPTC, and text metadata from published copies. Source images remain unchanged.
Metadata removal cannot replace visual review. Files in content/private are
withheld from the static site, but are not protected from a public source repo;
never put new sensitive originals in a repository intended to be public.

Ask: “Does this detail make the story meaningfully better, or does it mainly make
the person easier to identify?” Keep the former; omit, generalize, or ask before
publishing the latter. Follow explicit user approval for specific disclosures.
